Case studies: three turn-based / PvP indie accounts that grew on X, and what @Heroes0fcrypto copies

Read-only research, 14 Oct 2026, around 3:15-3:45 PM PT. About 25 X API calls and 3 web searches. Metrics are X API public_metrics at fetch time: L = likes, RT = reposts, R = replies, V = views.

API limits: read this first

  • No follower history. The API returns only today's follower count. Growth is reconstructed from milestone posts the accounts wrote themselves, from engagement on old posts, and from press.
  • No view counts before late 2022. Older posts show V = 0, meaning "not recorded", not zero views.
  • Search is a sample. Results are capped at 25 per call and sorted by relevancy, then re-ranked by likes + 2×RTs. A top-10 list here is the top 10 of what I sampled per era, not a full timeline census.
  • Mention counts aren't usable. get_posts_counts_recent for the game names came back at about 7,000-7,800 posts a day. "MENACE" and "Battle Brothers" are ordinary words, so that number is polluted and I don't use it.
  • @songsofconquest is the dedicated Songs of Conquest account. Press and Lavapotion retweets cite it, but it doesn't resolve via the API today: renamed, deactivated, or restricted. Unknown which.

Case 1: Super Auto Pets (@TeamWoodGames). The closest model for a free PvP browser game

How it started (Jan-May 2021): - First pitch, 30 Jan 2021, in the second post window: "To all of you who loves Hearthstone Battleground. We are making a similar game that contains way more muffins and way less stressful timers." 14 L. https://x.com/TeamWoodGames/status/1355562939567247366 - That's a comparison hook plus a pain point the audience already feels (stressful timers). - Early formats: - Screenshot Saturday clips ("Dear Deer. Revenge time!", 12 L) - "Who will win?" matchup clips (11 L) - A mascot comic series with Turtle (16-21 L each, Apr 2021) - Removing friction was announced as news: "you can now play online without having to sign up" (5 May 2021, 14 L) https://x.com/TeamWoodGames/status/1389929783128117255 - Free-to-play push "Play Super Auto Pets!" with video: 143 L / 23 R (9 May 2021) https://x.com/TeamWoodGames/status/1391344696518086656 - Friends multiplayer mode: "Play it here for free", 18 L (26 Jun 2021) https://x.com/TeamWoodGames/status/1408748122088542211

Case 2: Songs of Conquest (@Lavapotion). The HoMM-like genre match

How it started: - The oldest posts the search returns are from Jul-Aug 2019. The account was created in May 2019, so earlier posts may not be indexed. - It opened with world-building concept art written in-voice, e.g. "Official decree from empress Aurelia": 163 L (https://x.com/Lavapotion/status/1160871400909000704). A faction lore video got 149 L (https://x.com/Lavapotion/status/1159037208243970048). - A Reddit r/pcgaming AMA, announced on X (https://x.com/Lavapotion/status/1164451603270836224). - Many small, warm replies to individual users in the first months (dozens of 0-2 like "Thank you!" replies). - The publisher's interview says the 2019 trailer plus "art posts that have been trickling out on the twitter-account" and an active Discord drove exposure (Cubed3, https://www.cubed3.com/features/interviews/songs-of-conquest).

Inflection points: 1. Reached 10k followers by Dec 2020 (self-reported). 2. Early Access date run-up, Dec 2021-May 2022. This is the biggest cluster: - "BIG NEWS! Early Access in early Q2": 529 L / 91 RT - Trailer and date: 491 L - "How far we have come" comparisons: 702 L and 1,124 L 3. The 1.0 announcement, Apr 2024. Wishlists spiked to "almost half a million across Steam, GOG and EGS", per the studio's own post as reported by Game World Observer (https://gameworldobserver.com/2024/04/27/songs-of-conquest-500k-wishlists-1-0-launch). 500k copies were sold in week one (https://insider-gaming.com/songs-of-conquest-sells-500000-copies-announces-new-roadmap/). 4. The 2026 pivot to a new game (Wielders of the Essence) is carried by nostalgia and personality posts.

Case 3: Battle Brothers / MENACE (@OverhypeStudios). Slow, honest, dev-diary growth

How it started (2014): - Every post was a dev blog link plus a screenshot, with Screenshot Saturday used constantly: - "New #Battlebrothers Dev Blog on procedural character backgrounds" (6 L / 7 RT) - "Dont lose your head! now with gore galore! #ScreenshotSaturday" - A free demo announced on X (Apr 2014, https://x.com/OverhypeStudios/status/459644737838792704). - The best 2014 post got about 7 likes. - The devs' own story (Turn Based Lovers, https://turnbasedlovers.com/10-turns-interview/with-battle-brothers-developer/): it began as a hobby, "a medieval/fantasy version of the original X-com". Early Access in 2015 let them quit their jobs, with a core team of three. - Per a 2015 forum thread, coverage came from a Rock Paper Shotgun article plus "positive word of mouth... your efforts at documenting your thoughts... are generating a lot of confidence and goodwill" (http://battlebrothersgame.com/forums/topic/got-an-article-on-rps/). - Updates shipped every 1-3 weeks during Early Access, and they passed 50,000 sales by the March 2017 full release (ArchaeoGames interview).

Inflection points: - Battle Brothers DLC announces in 2019-2020: 114-195 L. - MENACE with publisher Hooded Horse. Hooded Horse's post got 501 L / 54,100 V (https://x.com/HoodedHorseInc/status/1995499666071253246). - The "250,000 copies sold" milestone: 764 L / 46,548 V (https://x.com/OverhypeStudios/status/2039332150537859328). This is their real number to share; we have no equivalent. - Player screenshot reposts with credit, e.g. "courtesy of Steam user BLASTER": 2,232 L / 70,456 V, their #1.

Contrast: @OneManLeft (PvP turn-based tactics, little X growth)

  • Hex Gambit was Kickstarted and covered by Nintendo press in 2018: Nintendo Everything footage got 6 L (https://x.com/NinEverything/status/980965785618141184).
  • The studio's own posts are mostly 0-6 L, e.g. faction statues at 1 L (https://x.com/OneManLeft/status/955606260904353792) and "multiplayer servers get set up, I'm drawin' pirates" at 2 L.
  • What went wrong:
  • Posts are announcements with no hook.
  • The game focus switches often: Tilt to Live, Outwitters, Hex Gambit, a pirate RPG, Mage Food Truck.
  • There's no creator or streamer loop.
  • Community was outsourced to a fan Facebook group.
  • PvP alone did not grow the account.

What we copy for @Heroes0fcrypto

Context: 90 followers, PvP, browser, not on Steam, under the XCOM.md bans (no hashtags, emoji, token talk, invented numbers).

  1. Make the free browser game the news, like SAP. "No sign-up / no install" was SAP's headline in 2021. For us it's "No install. No wallet to start." It's already in the pin, so keep it in the pin, not in every post.
  2. The comparison hook in the first post of a series, like SAP's "If you love Hearthstone Battlegrounds". For us: HoMM battles and Into the Breach, stated honestly. One concrete pain point, e.g. "the fight where you didn't check who acts next".
  3. Then-vs-now dev comparisons, Lavapotion's #1 format. Only with real old and new screenshots from the repo, and only describing what actually changed. Stepan supplies the facts; we never invent the history.
  4. The paired dev diary → "now live" cadence, like Overhype. One problem post, then one shipped post, in a flat factual voice. Map the problem to a news article on heroesofcrypto.io and put the link in the reply, per XCOM.md.
  5. Credited player boards, like Overhype's #1 and SAP's player-submitted weeklies. When a real player shares an end position or a clip, the human reposts or quotes it with credit. It's the top-performing format for both studios, and it's PvP-native: two humans, one board.
  6. The PvP creator loop, SAP's inflection. Small tactics streamers matter more than any post. The human can offer a match on stream; I can draft outreach but won't send it. Rewards are mentioned only when someone asks, per XCOM.md.
  7. Reply like early Lavapotion. Many short, warm, specific replies to every person who engages, especially anyone arguing about a move.
  8. Avoid the OneManLeft pattern. No unhooked announcements, no switching topics, and keep the community on X and on the site, not in a side channel.

Don't copy

  • SAP's mascot comics. They need emoji-free personality art we don't have.
  • Lavapotion's meme trends. Too far from "one board decision".
  • Sales milestones. We have none to share, and must not invent any.
  • Hashtag-heavy Screenshot Saturday posts, like early Overhype. A plain reply to a host works without the hashtag.
